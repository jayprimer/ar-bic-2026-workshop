Introduction
Globally, an estimated 5%–10% of the population is affected by immune-mediated diseases, and approxi-
mately one-third of those individuals have multiple autoimmune diseases.1–3 Increased understanding of
the molecular and cellular mechanisms underlying the pathogenesis of immune-mediated diseases has
enabled the development of targeted therapies against critical drivers of disease development.4 The tumor
necrosis factor (TNF) superfamily (TNFSF) ligands are major regulators of inflammatory responses
involved in the pathogenesis of numerous immune-mediated diseases.⁵
   TNF-like ligand 1A (TL1A), a member of the TNFSF, is a proinflammatory cytokine produced primarily
by myeloid, endothelial, epithelial, and activated T cells, as a type II transmembrane protein that can be
cleaved by membrane proteases (e.g., TNF-α–converting enzyme [TACE]) into its soluble form.6–13 Like
other archetypal TNFSF ligands, TL1A functions as a homotrimer that engages three molecules of its
cognate receptor, death receptor 3 (DR3), on the target cell surface.14,15 This clustering event triggers the
recruitment of the TNF receptor-associated death domain (TRADD) protein and subsequent activation of
nuclear factor kappa-light-chain-enhancer of activated B cells (NF-κB), amplifying inflammation.6,7,10

Additionally, TL1A can also bind to DcR3 (TNFSFR6B), which is a soluble decoy receptor that can inhibit
the activity of TNFSF members TL1A, FasL, and LIGHT.¹⁰
   When TL1A binds to DR3 on the surface of T cells, it acts as a stimulatory cytokine that enhances
differentiation and activation of multiple T helper (Th) subsets, including Th1, Th2, Th9, and Th17.6,7,16 An
overactive TL1A-DR3 pathway may increase the risk of autoimmunity by activating T cells, innate
lymphoid cells, and other immune and nonimmune cell types, leading to excessive cytokine production
and subsequent tissue damage.6,7 TL1A expression is increased in various immune-mediated diseases,
including ulcerative colitis (UC), Crohn’s disease, rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis
(PsA), axial spondyloarthritis (axSpA), and systemic lupus erythematosus.6,7,16–20 Emerging evidence from
translational studies also suggests upregulated TL1A – DR3 signaling in hidradenitis suppurativa.21 Genetic
variants in the TL1A gene (TNFSF15) are implicated in the pathogenesis of inflammatory bowel disease
(IBD) and various rheumatic diseases.16–20
   Despite the availability of multiple immunomodulatory therapies, a therapeutic ceiling persists across
various immune-mediated diseases, meaning current treatments often fail to achieve durable remission or
restore immune tolerance.22–25 These outcomes underscore the unmet need for more effective and durable
therapies. Targeting TL1A may offer a novel strategy by addressing immune activation pathways not
adequately controlled by existing therapies.6,7,26–29 In recent years, TL1A blockade has emerged as an
area of clinical development with three anti-TL1A antibodies (tulisokibart, afimkibart, duvakitug) demon-
strating efficacy in Phase 2 clinical trials for the treatment of UC or Crohn’s disease.26–29
   SPY002 and SPY072 are extended half-life (t1/2), fully human immunoglobulin (Ig) G1 anti-TL1A
antibodies being developed for IBD and rheumatic diseases, respectively, with the potential to reduce
dosing frequency compared to tulisokibart, afimkibart, and duvakitug, which are non-half-life extended
antibodies.26–29 Here, we report in vitro and in vivo characterization of SPY002 and SPY072, including
analyses of binding affinity, specificity, structure, as well as pharmacodynamics (PD), pharmacokinetics
(PK), and toxicology studies to evaluate safety and effectiveness in animal models.

Material and methods
Isolation of anti-TL1A antibodies from immunized transgenic mice
To generate fully human antibodies against TL1A, ATX-Gx™ mice (Alloy Therapeutics) were immunized.
Cohorts of mice were immunized with weekly doses of recombinant TL1A (Acro Biosystems; TLA-H5243)
alone or alternating weekly doses of recombinant TL1A and a proprietary DNA vector encoding the human
TL1A gene (Antibody Solutions).
   For titer measurement, mouse sera were prepared and assessed in an enzyme-linked immunosorbent
assay (ELISA) using the same recombinant TL1A used for immunization. Once sufficient titers were
achieved, mice were sacrificed and lymph nodes were harvested.
   As a strategy to identify antibodies against TL1A, lymph nodes harvested from immunized mice were
processed for single B-cell screening, using proprietary AbTheneum™ technology (Single Cell Technology).
Briefly, cluster of differentiation (CD) 138+ cells were isolated from lymph nodes and screened for binding
against recombinant TL1A. Individual B cells from wells with a positive signal were subsequently isolated
and sequenced using next-generation sequencing.
   As another strategy to identify antibodies against TL1A, lymph nodes were harvested from immunized
mice and messenger RNA was isolated to build an immune phage library expressing antigen-binding
fragments (Fab). Periplasmic extracts were screened for binding to recombinant TL1A by surface plasmon
resonance (SPR) and to Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells overexpressing TL1A. The heavy and light
chain variable regions of SPY002 and SPY072 were identified using 1 of the 2 aforementioned strategies.

SPR measurement of binding
The binding of SPY002 and SPY072 to the TL1A antigen was analyzed using the BIAcore 8K instrument
(GE Healthcare). The experimental format used was single- or multi-cycle kinetics. Streptavidin was
immobilized on a BIAcore Series S CM5 sensor chip using standard amine coupling chemistry.

A biotinylated anti-human IgG crystallizable fragment (Fc) region antibody (CaptureSelect, ThermoFisher
7,103,262,100) was loaded onto the streptavidin sensor chip. The chip was then loaded with SPY002 or

pH 1.5) was injected with a flow rate of 50 μL/minute and a contact time of 60 seconds.
   For testing antibody cross-reactivity to TNFSF proteins, a similar SPR method was used, except
a BIAcore Series S Sensor Chip Protein G (Cytiva; 29,179,315) was used and loaded with SPY002 or
SPY072 in HBS-EP+ running buffer.
   For Fc gamma receptor (FcγR), neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn), and complement component 1q (C1q) SPR
assays, the capture reagent (CaptureSelect human Fab kappa kinetics biotin conjugate [ThermoFisher;
7,103,302,100] for FcγR and FcRn; CaptureSelect biotin anti–IgG-Fc [human] conjugate [ThermoFisher;
7,103,262,500] for C1q) was bound to the SPR flow cell via streptavidin. Test antibodies were diluted to
5 μg/mL and injected into the SPR flow cell. Antibodies were bound to the capture reagent via the Fab region
for FcγR and FcRn assays and via the Fc region for the C1q assay. FcγRs (Rouken Bio, His Tag, produced in
HEK293), FcRn (Rouken Bio, His Tag, produced in HEK293), and C1q (Sigma, C1740) binding to the test
antibodies were then measured. The equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) was calculated for each receptor
when binding was observed.

DR3 and decoy receptor 3 ELISA assays
The potency of SPY002 and SPY072 inhibition of soluble TL1A binding to DR3 or decoy receptor 3 (DcR3)
was measured using ELISA. An ELISA plate was coated in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) with 100 μL of
1 μg/mL of human DcR3 (Acro Biosystems; TNB-H5255, Fc tag) or 2 μg/mL of human DR3 (ChemPartner,
AA25-199, His Tag) and incubated overnight at 4°C. After incubation, each well was washed 3 times with
wash buffer (PBS +0.05% Tween20), and 300 μL of blocking buffer (PBS +0.05% Tween20 + 1% bovine
serum albumin [BSA]) was added to each well and incubated at 37°C for 2 hours. Wells were washed 3 times
after incubation. A mixture of biotinylated soluble, trimer TL1A (175 μL of 4 ng/mL [DR3 assay] and 0.2 ng/
mL [DcR3 assay]; Acro Biosystems; TLA-H52Q1) and SPY002 or SPY072 (175 μL at concentrations ranging
from 0 to 3 nM [DR3 assay] and 0 to 50 nM [DcR3 assay]) was prepared and incubated for 30 minutes at
room temperature (RT). The above biotin – TL1A/SPY002 or biotin – TL1A/SPY072 mixture (100 μL) was
added to each well and incubated at 37°C for 1 hour. Wells were washed 3 times with wash buffer, and
100 μL of detection reagent (in blocking buffer) was added to each well and incubated at 37°C for 1 hour.
Wells were washed 3 times with wash buffer after incubation, and 100 μL of 3,3‘,5,5’-tetramethylbenzidine
was added to each well and incubated for 10 minutes at RT for color development. Stop solution (50 μL of
1N HCl) was added to each plate prior to absorbance measurement at 450 nm. Each experiment was run in
triplicate.

Flow cytometry assay
SPY002 and SPY072 binding potency to human membrane TL1A was measured by flow cytometry
using a stable Raji cell line engineered to express human TL1A. Raji cells were suspended at
a density of 2 × 106 cells/mL, and 100 μL of cell suspension was plated in a 96-well plate at 2 ×
105 cells/well. Next, 100 μL/well of 2-times concentrated SPY002 and SPY072 antibody (5-point
titration with 10-fold dilutions starting at a high concentration of 50 μg/mL) was added and
incubated for 30 minutes at 2 − 8°C. Plates were washed and then incubated with allophycocyanin-
conjugated anti-human IgG-Fc secondary antibody (Biolegend, 366,906) for 30 minutes at 2 − 8°C.
The plate was washed and analyzed by flow cytometry. The median fluorescence intensity (MFI) was
plotted for each concentration of antibody, and the data were fit with a 4-parameter sigmoidal
curve.

Human erythroleukemia cell line apoptosis assay
Human erythroleukemia cell line (TF-1) was cultured in Roswell Park Memorial Institute 1640
medium (RPMI-1640) containing 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 2 ng/mL granulocyte-
macrophage colony-stimulating factor. Prior to performing the assay, TF-1 cells were harvested
and washed twice with PBS. The cells were resuspended in RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS at 8 × 10⁵
cells/mL, and 25 μL/well was dispensed into the assay plate. SPY002 or SPY072 antibody was
diluted to a 4x stock solution (200 nM) and serially diluted 7 times with 3-fold dilutions. Twenty-
five μL/well of each antibody dilution was added in quintuplicate to the assay plate. Next, 25 μL of
each 4x stock solution of cycloheximide (CHX) at 1 μg/mL and TL1A at 25 ng/mL was added to the
wells. The plate was incubated at 37°C for 6 hours. Caspase-Glo® 3/7 reagent (Promega; G8092) was
added at 50 μL/well, mixed using a plate shaker for 1 minute and then incubated for 30 minutes at
RT. Luminescence, corresponding to caspase activation and apoptosis, was then measured using
a SpectraMax M5 microplate (Molecular Devices, LLC) reader set to 500 ms/well and plotted
against the concentration of SPY002 or SPY072 in the well. Control wells received no treatment,
CHX only, or TL1A with CHX in the absence of SPY002 or SPY072. The percent inhibition of
apoptosis was calculated by averaging the luminescence signal in the presence of TL1A and CHX
without antibody added (LumMax) and the luminescence signal in the presence of CHX only
(LumMin); the formula used for calculation: ([LumMax-LumSample]/[LumMax-LumMin]) × 100%.
Data were plotted in GraphPad Prism and each curve was fit with a 4PL sigmoidal curve to
determine the IC50 values.


SPY002 and SPY072 nonhuman primate PK assays
PK assessment after a single subcutaneous (SC) administration of 25 mg/mL SPY002 at 50 mg/kg was
conducted in male (n = 5/group) cynomolgus monkeys over 98 days. PK assessment after a single SC
administration of 25 mg/mL SPY072 at 50 mg/kg was conducted in male (n = 5/group) cynomolgus
monkeys over 91 days. The dose of 50 mg/kg was chosen to give serum concentration above those that
would show target-mediated drug disposition (TMDD) and anti-drug antibodies so that accurate PK
parameters could be calculated. Blood samples were collected via cephalic or saphenous veins. Blood
samples were placed in polyethylene tubes without anticoagulant and allowed to stand at RT for about
1 hour until centrifuged. After centrifugation, the serum was aliquoted then placed in dry ice immediately
and remained frozen until determination of the serum concentrations of the test article. Serum samples
were transferred to −80°C for long term storage. Serial PK sampling was performed at predose; 10 minutes;
1, 4, 8, and 24 hours; and 2, 4, 7, 14, 21, 28, 35, 42, 49, 56, 63, 77, 91 and 98 (SPY002 only) days postdose (19
time points).
   Sensitive and selective ligand binding assays were used for the serum sample analysis of SPY002 and
SPY072 in nonhuman primate (cynomolgus monkey) studies. SPY002 was formulated at 160 mg/mL, 80
mg/mL, and 25 mg/mL in a buffer containing histidine, arginine, EDTA, and polysorbate 80 and sub-
cutaneously administered at doses of 160 mg/kg, 80 mg/kg, and 25 mg/kg. SPY072 was formulated at 160
mg/mL, 80 mg/mL, and 25 mg/mL in a buffer containing histidine, arginine, EDTA, and polysorbate 80 and
subcutaneously administered at doses of 160 mg/kg, 80 mg/kg, and 25 mg/kg. Blood samples were collected,
processed into serum, and analyzed for SPY002 and SPY072 concentrations. Analysis of PK study samples
was performed via a non-good laboratory practice (non-GLP) assay that had a lower limit of quantitation of
78.1 ng/mL for SPY002 and SPY072. Analysis of 26-week toxicology study samples was performed using
validated GLP assays that had a lower limit of quantitation of 200 ng/mL for SPY002 and SPY072. Assays
were tested for selectivity by spiking TL1A samples with cynomolgus serum matrix from 10 individual
monkeys and demonstrating no interference in the assay. PK and toxicokinetic (TK) parameters (Tmax,
Cmax, area under the curve [AUC], t1/2, R) were determined using noncompartmental analysis on all
individual concentration – time profiles and means reported.
   PK analyses were performed on serum concentration versus time data using Phoenix WinNonlin (v8.4)
noncompartmental analysis function (linear trapezoidal/linear interpolation method for AUC calculations).
Nominal dose values and sampling times were used for calculations.

SPY002 and SPY072 quantitative systems pharmacology modeling
Cynomolgus monkey PK parameters for SPY002 and SPY072 were used to estimate the drug PK parameters
that were scaled to the human model. Cynomolgus monkey and human quantitative systems pharmacology
(QSP) models were developed to describe the effect of the drug on TL1A concentrations. The models have
a similar structure, but parameter values are species-specific. The models are composed of central (blood)
and peripheral (tissues) compartments, with a gut compartment as the site of action for human modeling.
The compartment volumes were defined based on physiologically relevant values of plasma and tissue
interstitium (for both peripheral and gut compartments). The models include dosing of anti-TL1A anti-
bodies through intravenous (IV) or SC administration, intercompartmental distribution, catabolic elimina-
tion, and binding to membrane-bound and soluble trimeric TL1A and soluble monomeric TL1A. Synthesis
and degradation is modeled for all protein species: membrane and soluble TL1A (monomeric and trimeric)
and binding partners, DR3 and DcR3. All model reactions are described using zeroth-, first-, or second-
order mass action kinetics. TMDD was incorporated into the QSP model using a Michaelis-Menton
elimination function, and TMDD parameters were determined by fitting the model to published human
clinical trial PK data for an anti-TL1A antibody.³⁰
   Protein-specific parameters, such as concentration, half-life, and turnover rate were determined from
literature measurements when available. Soluble target concentrations were parameterized by plasma
measurements in relevant patient populations. Soluble target half-life was input based on published
literature values or calibrated to data if available. Membrane protein concentrations were calculated using
“bottom-up” methods. Protein expression was calculated using the sum of the number of cells for each cell
type expressing the protein, the proportion of each cell type expressing the protein, and the number of
protein molecules per cell. Membrane target turnover rates were identified from in vitro cell line measure-
ments when available. When data were not available, assumptions based on other proteins of the same
family, similar structure, molecular weight, or function were used. All models were implemented using
Applied BioMath QSP Notebook platform version 2.0.0b12.

Immunoassay for detection and quantification of TL1A
A sandwich immunoassay was developed in which 0.50 μg/mL biotinylated capture antibody (SPY002 or
SPY072) was immobilized on a streptavidin-coated plate by incubating for 60 ± 6 minutes with shaking (600
revolutions per minute) at RT. Plates were washed and blocked before addition of samples containing
monomeric and/or trimeric human TL1A (Acro Biosystems; TLA-H5243). Samples were then incubated at
RT for 60 ± 6 minutes with shaking at RT. Next, 1.5 μg/mL of a noncompeting ruthenylated anti-TL1A
detection antibody was added and incubated on the plate at RT for 60 ± 6 minutes with shaking. The plate
was then washed and the electrochemiluminescent signal was read by a Meso Scale Discovery Sector Imager
S600 reader.

Size-exclusion chromatography coupled to multi-angle light scattering
Purified SPY002 Fab or SPY072 Fab was incubated at a 1:1 molar ratio (9 μM) with human TL1A
(PeproTech; 310–23) at 37°C for 60 minutes. Size-exclusion chromatography coupled to multi-angle light
scattering (SEC-MALS) was performed by an Agilent 1260 high-performance liquid chromatography
system with a MALS detector (Wyatt) containing a TSKgel G2000SWxL (Tosoh Bioscience) column. The
mobile phase consisted of 200 mM disodium phosphate, 1% isopropyl alcohol (pH 7.0), at a flow rate of 0.5
mL/minute with a 30-minute run time. Results were analyzed by ASTRA software (Wyatt).

FabALACTICA kit (Genovis; A2-AFK) and buffer exchanged into the same crystallization buffer. TL1A/
SPY002 Fab and TL1A/SPY072 Fab complexes were formed by mixing TL1A and SPY002 Fab or SPY072

Fab at a 1:1.2 molar ratio. The complex was subsequently purified on a HiLoad Superdex 200 column
(Cytiva). The purified complex was concentrated to approximately 10 mg/mL and used for all crystallization
experiments. Initial crystallization trials of the complex were carried out using the sitting drop method on
a Formulatrix NT8 robot at 20°C using commercial sparse-matrix conditions. This led to the identification

cryoprotected in a solution containing 2.1 M ammonium sulfate, 0.1 M sodium acetate (pH 5.0), and 10%
glycerol. For SPY072, crystals grew to >50 μm in the longest dimension. The crystal that provided the data
set used to obtain the structure of the complex was cryoprotected in a solution containing 0.1 M sodium
citrate tribasic dihydrate (pH 5.5) and 16% polyethylene glycol 8000.
   The diffraction data at a resolution of 2.21 Å for SPY002 and 2.34 Å for SPY072 were collected at
a cryogenic temperature (100 K) using the 8.3.1 beamline at the Advanced Light Source (Chicago) with 0.2°
oscillation/image. Data reduction was carried out in Xia2 (Collaborative Computational Project Number 4
[CCP4]) using the Diffraction Integration for Advanced Light Sources pipeline. For SPY002, molecular
replacement was carried out on the 3.79 Å data set with Phaser (CCP4) using the structures of TL1A
(Protein Data Bank [PDB] ID: 3QE3), heavy chain of the Fab (PDB ID: 5 × 8 M), and light chain of the Fab
(PDB ID: 5IBT) as search models. A single solution was obtained with a log-likelihood gain (LLG) score of
581 and a translation function Z (TFZ) score of 19.4. For SPY072, molecular replacement was carried out
with Phaser (CCP4) using the structures of TL1A (PDB ID: 3QE3) and the Fab (PDB ID: 8AS0) as search
models. A single solution was obtained with an LLG score of 901 and a TFZ score of 15.4. Initially, 100
cycles of rigid body refinement were carried out using REFMAC5 (CCP4). Subsequently, 100 cycles of
restrained refinement were carried out using REFMAC5 (CCP4). Several cycles of manual rebuilding in the
Crystallographic Object-Oriented Toolkit and restrained refinement using REFMAC5 (CCP4) were carried
out. Water molecules were added to the model, and a final round of restrained and translation/libration/
screw refinement was carried out using REFMAC5. The final working R-factor (Rwork) and free R-factor
(Rfree) values for SPY002 were 0.227 and 0.233, respectively. The final Rwork and Rfree values for SPY072
were 0.233 and 0.266, respectively.
   Both SPY002 and SPY072 crystal structures were similar to the results of structures solved by cryogenic
electron microscopy (cryo-EM) but with better resolution (data not shown).

In vivo safety assessments of SPY002 and SPY072
Toxicity studies were conducted in cynomolgus monkeys in accordance with International Council
for Harmonization of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (guidelines M3
[R2], S6[R1]), and other relevant guidelines.31,32 The purpose of the toxicity studies was to evaluate
the safety of SPY002 and SPY072 at exposures exceeding those anticipated in envisioned clinical
studies. Dose range finding studies were conducted to inform the design of subsequent GLP
toxicity assessments (data not shown). Potential toxicities attributed to dosing SPY002 or SPY072
to cynomolgus monkeys via the IV or SC routes were evaluated in 6-week and 26-week GLP
studies. Cynomolgus monkeys were administered vehicle control (20 mM histidine, 125 mM argi-
nine, 0.05 mM EDTA, 0.02% [w/v], polysorbate 80, pH 6.0) or SPY002/SPY072 at doses up to 160
mg/kg SC or IV every 2 weeks (Q2W) for 6 weeks or up to 160 mg/kg SC Q2W or IV monthly for
26 weeks. Drug-naïve monkeys (3 males and 3 females per group in the main study groups, with 2
males and 2 females in the recovery groups) from 2 to 3 years of age were dosed with 0, 15, 50, or
160 mg/mL SPY002 or SPY072 at 1 mL/kg for the 6-week study. Drug-naïve monkeys (4 males and
4 females per group in the main study groups, with 2 males and 2 females in the recovery groups)
from 2 to 3 years of age were dosed with 0, 25, 80, or 160 mg/mL SPY002 or SPY072 at 1 mL/kg for
the 26-week study. Toxicology endpoints included mortality, clinical observations, body weight,
qualitative food consumption, veterinary examinations, ophthalmology, quantitative and qualitative

SPY002 and SPY072 are selective, high-affinity binders of human TL1A
Binding kinetics and affinity of SPY002 and SPY072 toward soluble human TL1A were measured by SPR

affinity toward human trimeric TL1A with KD values of 35 pM and 31 pM, respectively (Table 1). These
values indicated that both antibodies bound tightly to TL1A despite subtle differences in their kinetic
binding properties. Both antibodies exhibited negligible binding to related TNFSF ligands TNF, Fas ligand
(FasL), TNF-related apoptosis-inducing ligand (TRAIL), and TNFSF14 (LIGHT) in SPR assays, demon-

affinities of 290 pM and 810 pM, respectively.

SPY002 and SPY072 are potent TL1A antagonists
Both SPY002 and SPY072 showed concentration-dependent inhibition of soluble TL1A binding to plate-
bound DR3, with potencies of 0.054 nM and 0.069 nM, respectively (Figure 1A); similarly, both antibodies
blocked the binding of soluble TL1A to plate-bound DcR3, with potencies of 0.32 nM and 0.83 nM,
respectively (Figure 1B).
   In a cell-based assay, SPY002 and SPY072 inhibited the binding of soluble trimeric TL1A to the surface of
DR3-expressing CHO cells, with potencies of 4.4 nM and 5.1 nM, respectively (Figure 1C). Moreover, both
SPY002 and SPY072 inhibited soluble TL1A-enhanced apoptosis in TF-1 cells, a human erythroleukemia
cell line that expresses high levels of endogenous DR3, with inhibitory potencies of 0.11 nM and 0.095 nM,
respectively (Figure 1D). These antibodies also inhibited soluble TL1A-induced IFN-γ secretion in a whole
blood assay with mean potencies of 0.11 nM (SPY002) and 0.052 nM (SPY072) using four healthy human
donors (Figure S2). These results suggested that both SPY002 and SPY072 could functionally inhibit soluble
TL1A cellular activity with subnanomolar potency.
   Finally, the ability of SPY002 and SPY072 to bind transmembrane TL1A was measured. Both SPY002 and
SPY072 bound to the surface of Raji cells expressing full-length human TL1A and exhibited
a concentration-dependent MFI signal with a half-maximal effective concentration of 0.27 nM and 0.19
nM, respectively (Figure 1E). Taken together, these results suggested that SPY002 and SPY072 are potent
inhibitors of human TL1A and could bind to both soluble and cell membrane-bound forms of TL1A.

Anti-TL1A is efficacious in a mouse TNBS colitis model
Intrarectal administration of TNBS in rodents rapidly induces colitis and is a common mouse model used to
test therapeutics for IBD.33 A potent and selective anti-TL1A antibody (SPY002-089) with identical CDRs to

antibody (SPY003-104) and an anti-TNF antibody (adalimumab, produced based upon the publicly
available sequence). Due to the lack of cross-reactivity of SPY002-089 and SPY003-104 with mouse
orthologs, mice expressing human TL1A and human IL-23 in place of the mouse proteins were used in
this study.

Discussion
SPY002 and SPY072 are two novel monoclonal antibodies that target the TL1A-DR3 signaling pathway by
binding to TL1A. These investigational antibodies demonstrated similar primary pharmacology (e.g., binding
affinity, on-target potency) consistent with potent human TL1A binding and neutralization (KD ≤ 1 nM). Both
molecules bound to recombinant soluble human TL1A and cell surface TL1A with high affinity and did not
cross-react with TL1A-related TNFSF members TNF, FasL, TRAIL, or LIGHT, indicating that they act
selectively on TL1A. In assays evaluating TL1A-induced cellular function, SPY002 and SPY072 demonstrated
similar inhibition of apoptosis in TF-1 cells and inhibited TL1A-mediated IFN-γ secretion in a human whole
blood assay. These data demonstrate that SPY002 and SPY072 can block TL1A from binding to its cellular
receptor DR3 to prevent downstream cellular signaling.
   Despite the similarities in binding potency and functional inhibition of SPY002 and SPY072 for
TL1A, there were biochemical properties that differentiated SPY002 and SPY072. Although both
antibodies were capable of binding and inhibiting trimeric human TL1A in cell-based assays, SEC-
MALS data showed that SPY072 Fab was observed to be bound primarily to dissociated monomeric
TL1A subunits, suggesting that SPY072 binds monomeric TL1A and dissociates trimeric TL1A into
antibody-bound TL1A monomers. An ELISA assay confirmed that SPY072 binds to the trimeric and
monomeric forms of TL1A equally well, whereas SPY002 shows preferential binding to the trimer.
Structural data suggest that there is significant overlap in the TL1A epitope recognized by SPY002 and
SPY072. However, SPY002 appears to interact with its cognate TL1A monomeric subunit and with an
adjacent monomeric subunit within the TL1A trimer, thereby providing a structural explanation for its
preferential binding to trimeric TL1A over monomeric TL1A. As an archetypal member of TNFSF,
trimeric TL1A induces activation of the DR3 receptor complex, while monomeric TL1A cannot activate
DR3 signaling.15,36 Therefore, because both SPY002 and SPY072 appear to be functionally equivalent at
binding and inhibiting trimeric TL1A, both are clinically viable options for treating TL1A-mediated
diseases.
   The FcRn binding properties of SPY002 and SPY072 were consistent with expectations for YTE-modified
antibodies, showing higher affinity for FcRn at an acidic, endosomal pH relative to a wild-type control
        37
antibody but no binding to FcRn at neutral pH. In cynomolgus monkeys, the t1/2 of SPY002 was
approximately 23 days and that of SPY072 was approximately 27 days, consistent with the extended t1/2
expected for YTE-engineered antibodies.37,38 The monkey PK data were utilized to create a QSP model,
which predicted human t1/2 values of 76.9 and 87.6 days for SPY002 and SPY072, respectively. These
predictions were in-line with subsequent SPY002 and SPY072 phase 1 human PK data showing t1/2 values
approximately 3 times that of typical non–YTE-modified IgG1 antibodies.39,40 Thus, the PK properties of
SPY002 and SPY072 align with the potential for quarterly or biannual dosing regimens (i.e., every 3 months
or every 6 months).39,40
   Although Phase 2 clinical proof of concept has been demonstrated with three different anti-TL1A
antibodies in IBD, preclinical pharmacology data supporting expansion of anti-TL1A therapy into other
indications is less abundant.26–29 Anti-TL1A treatment using antibodies with CDRs similar to SPY072 in
a rat arthritis model and mouse psoriasis model resulted in responses that were comparable to clinically
validated anti-TNF and/or anti–IL-23 treatments. These data support a potential role for TL1A in inflam-
matory rheumatic and dermatologic diseases, consistent with its observed upregulation in RA, axSpA, PsA,
and psoriasis,7 and further support investigation of anti-TL1A therapy beyond IBD in other inflammatory
diseases, especially those affecting the joints and skin.28
   SPY002 and SPY072 are being tested in Phase 1 studies (NCT06672718; NCT06622070) to deter-
mine the safety, tolerability, PK, and PD of single ascending doses in healthy volunteers. SPY002 is also
being evaluated as part of the Phase 2 SKYLINE-UC trial (NCT07012395) to determine the efficacy and
safety of the antibody in adults with moderately to severely active UC. SPY072 is being evaluated in the
Phase 2 SKYWAY-RD trial (NCT07148414) in patients with moderately to severely active RA, PsA, or
axSpA.

   In conclusion, SPY002 and SPY072 are potent anti-TL1A monoclonal antibodies with favorable phar-
macological and safety profiles as observed in preclinical models. Both antibodies demonstrate strong and
selective binding to TL1A and block TL1A binding to its receptor DR3. Their extended t1/2 and high safety
margins support quarterly or biannual dosing and the potential for safe clinical use. Collectively, these
findings provide a strong rationale for further clinical development of SPY002 and SPY072 in immune-
mediated diseases.
